A particle becomes a cloud droplet only if the air is humid enough. The threshold depends on its size.

The question

How readily an aerosol particle activates into a cloud droplet depends on how large it is and what it is made of. Get that relationship wrong and climate models get clouds wrong, which is one of the largest remaining uncertainties in them. My work is on the measurement end of the problem: making the numbers that come off an aerosol bench trustworthy enough to answer it.

Experimental

Size-selected CCN activation

A differential mobility analyzer pulls a single particle size out of a polydisperse stream. That monodisperse flow is split in two: a condensation particle counter counts every particle present, and a CCN-100 counter counts only the ones that activate into droplets at a set supersaturation. The ratio of the two, scanned across sizes and supersaturations, gives the activation curve and the critical diameter.

Method

Correcting for chamber wall loss

During a chamber experiment, particles are continuously lost to the walls by diffusion and settling. The measured distribution decays over the run even when nothing chemically changes, and because the loss rate depends on particle size, the shape of the distribution distorts rather than simply scaling down. Every quantity derived from it inherits that distortion.

I fit an eddy-diffusivity wall-loss model to the SMPS scan series and apply the correction per size bin, so the reported distribution describes the aerosol that was suspended rather than the fraction that reached the inlet.

Software

Making the data usable

Two instruments recording the same experiment rarely agree on what time it is, and a few seconds of offset is enough to smear an activation curve. The lab's analysis app, which I lead the development of, takes that over along with the rest of the routine handling:

  • Detects new instrument files as they are written and identifies the run they belong to
  • Corrects clock offsets between instruments before anything is merged
  • Joins CCN records to their matching DMA and CPC records into one analyzed run
  • Keeps every processed run in a searchable library, so past results are reopened rather than rederived
  • Streams live readings from a LabJack on a Raspberry Pi during an experiment
