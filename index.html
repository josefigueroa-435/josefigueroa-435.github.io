I measure the particles that turn water vapour into clouds — and write the software that makes those measurements hold up.

About

I am an undergraduate chemical engineering student at UC Riverside and a researcher in Dr. Sarah Petters' aerosol laboratory at CE-CERT. On the bench I run size-selected cloud condensation nuclei experiments: a differential mobility analyzer picks one particle size, a condensation particle counter counts everything, and a CCN counter counts only what becomes a droplet.

The other half of the job is software. Instruments disagree about the time, particles vanish to chamber walls mid-experiment, and results that took a week to produce get lost in a folder. I built the lab's analysis app to close those gaps, and most of what I know about engineering judgement came from doing it.

Before this I spent ten years as a certified pharmacy technician, where a careless result has a person on the other end of it. That is still how I think about data.

SMPS chamber wall-loss correction

Method development

Particles are lost to the chamber walls at a size-dependent rate, which distorts the shape of every distribution measured. An eddy-diffusivity fit corrects it per size bin.
