I work on cloud condensation nuclei — the aerosol particles that water vapour needs in order to become a cloud droplet. My interest is in the measurement itself: what an aerosol instrument actually reports, what it quietly gets wrong, and what has to be corrected before a number means anything.

About

I am an undergraduate in chemical engineering at UC Riverside, graduating in June 2027, and a student researcher in Dr. Sarah Petters' aerosol laboratory at CE-CERT. On the bench I run size-selected CCN activation experiments. Away from it, I develop the lab's analysis software, which is where most of the subtleties live: instruments that disagree about the time, particles lost to chamber walls mid-experiment, results that have to be found again a year later.

I came to engineering after ten years as a certified pharmacy technician, a decade of work where a careless result has a person on the other end of it. That shaped how I treat data more than any course has.

I am applying to chemical and environmental engineering doctoral programs for fall 2027.

Current work

Size-selected CCN activation

Petters aerosol laboratory, CE-CERT · 2026–present

Activation measurements on a CCN-100 counter paired with a differential mobility analyzer and a condensation particle counter, scanning across particle size and supersaturation.

Wall-loss correction for chamber size distributions

Method development · 2026

An eddy-diffusivity model fitted to SMPS scan series, correcting the size-dependent loss of particles to chamber walls so that reported distributions describe what was suspended.

Analysis software for the laboratory

Python, Streamlit, LabJack · 2026–present

Automatic instrument file detection, clock-offset correction between instruments, merging of CCN and DMA/CPC runs, a searchable library of processed results, and live streaming from a LabJack on a Raspberry Pi.
